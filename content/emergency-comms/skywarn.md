## BRUNSWICK SKYWARN

When severe or tropical weather threatens Brunswick County, the Brunswick County

Skywarn™ Emergency Coordinators (ECs) will activate the Skywarn™ spotters in

Brunswick County. Per the revised spotter reporting procedure from ILM NWS, the

spotters will report to the ECs the weather event information. The ECs will enter the

spotter’s report in the NWS chat system **(The NWS system is called ‘SLACK’)**. This

method is currently being used nation-wide.

This procedure was put in place by NWS and speeds up the reports to NWS, it utilizes

current technology which has proven to be more effective in getting reports into NWS

promptly. Spotters can also monitor the Wilmington NWS hazardous weather outlook

webpage to see if spotter activation will be necessary, or self-activate if conditions are

warranted in their area. The Brunswick County ECs are Dean (N4TYS) and David

(KN4UKU).

Reporting from the spotters to the ECs can be done using the groupme app as a

primary communication method. This has proven to work well for reporting and

transferring photos. Also in development is the VARAC FM chat mode on frequency

146.55 (simplex). The VARAC mode allows Skywarn™ to continue to operate and

communicate in the event that all infrastructure goes down (power, cell towers,

internet). As the VARAC framework is currently being developed and deployed, we will

use the groupme chat room, phone call to one of the ECs or email as an additional

method of reporting to the ECs as long as those methods are available.

Spotters can also utilize SSB HF frequencies in the HF bands, as well as VARAC HF

mode, FM Simplex, Winlink and the Brunswick Skywarn GroupMe chat room (download the GroupMe app for your phone or on a pc <a href="https://www.groupme.com" target="_blank">www.groupme.com</a>). Search for **Brunswick Skywarn** and ask to join the chat room.

ECs will alert spotters what mode and frequency to use at time of activation.

### What to report:

* What/When/Where (must have this information in your report)

* Funnel cloud, tornado, or waterspout

* Winds over 40 mph,

* Damage to buildings and/or trees

* Hail of any size (include a photo!)

* Flooding - describe if standing or flowing

* Any weather-related injuries or fatalities

* Photos are encouraged as part of a spotter report.
