# Choose Your License: The Three Tiers of Amateur Radio

Getting your FCC amateur radio license is straightforward, but knowing which milestone you are aiming for helps you study with a purpose. The licensing system is divided into three progressive tiers. Each exam unlocks a massive new set of frequencies, operating modes, and global capabilities.

Here is a breakdown of the three license classes, what to expect at a testing session, and the best way to ace your exam.

---

## 🎓 The Three Licensing Tiers

### 1. The Technician Class (The Entry Point)
This is the mandatory starting point for every new operator. 
* **The Exam:** 35 multiple-choice questions covering basic radio regulations, elementary electronics safety, and basic operating rules. You need a **74% (26 out of 35 correct)** to pass.
* **What It Unlocks:** Full operating privileges on all amateur bands above 30 MHz. This includes the local VHF/UHF repeaters used by our club, tactical simplex channels, amateur satellites, and a dedicated sliver of single-sideband (SSB) voice on the **10-Meter HF band** for entry-level worldwide DX chasing.

### 2. The General Class (The Sweet Spot)
This is where the real meat of the hobby lives. Once you pass your Technician exam, you can immediately take the General test at the same session for no extra fee if you are ready.
* **The Exam:** 35 multiple-choice questions focusing more on HF propagation, basic antenna design, circuit components, and specialized operating protocols. 
* **What It Unlocks:** Access to over **80% of the entire HF spectrum**. This hands you widespread access to the legendary global "money bands"—including 20-Meters, 40-Meters, and 80-Meters—allowing you to reliably communicate around the world without relying on internet links or local repeaters.

### 3. The Amateur Extra Class (The Apex Tier)
The highest license tier available in amateur radio. It is widely considered a badge of honor and a significant technical achievement.
* **The Exam:** A more rigorous 50 multiple-choice questions diving deep into advanced radio frequency engineering, circuit designs, radio wave physics, and international operating rules.
* **What It Unlocks:** Exclusive access to the bottom **25 kHz DX windows** on the HF bands. This gives you first pick at rare international DX stations, less crowded contesting zones, pristine voice slots, and full Volunteer Examiner (VE) privileges to grade and sign off on any incoming ham exam tier.

---

## 📝 What to Expect on Testing Day

Testing sessions are organized locally by groups of certified Volunteer Examiners (VEs) like the team right here at our club. 

* **The Format:** The exams are drawn from public, standardized question pools. There are absolutely no trick questions or surprise curveballs. 
* **No Morse Code Required:** The FCC officially dropped all Morse Code (CW) testing requirements decades ago. The modern exam is **100% written multiple-choice.**
* **The Single-Sitting Advantage (The Double-Barrel Pass):** If you pass your Technician exam, the VE team will immediately ask if you want to try the General exam right then and there. **There is no extra fee to take the next test in the same session.** As long as you keep passing, you can keep climbing the tiers in a single sitting!

## 📚 Recommended Study Strategy: Aim for the General

Because of how the FCC structures the question banks, **we highly recommend studying for both the Technician and General exams at the same time.** There is an immense amount of concept crossover between the two pools regarding basic radio safety, operating protocols, and elementary electronics rules. If you are already memorizing the math for your Technician ticket, you are already halfway to earning your General. 

1. **Grasp the Overlap:** Use your study manuals to look at basic electronics formulas and wave frequencies simultaneously for both tiers. 
2. **Drill the Real Questions:** Transition to **[HamStudy.org](https://hamstudy.org/)**. This is the absolute gold standard reference tool for ham testing. It tracks your progress using smart flashcards and adaptive practice exams. Use it to track your Tech scores, and once you are hitting a solid 85%+ pass rate, start throwing General practice tests into your rotation.
3. **Play the Percentages (Don't Fight the Outliers):** When drilling the question pools, you will inevitably run into a few specific questions or complex equations that always throw you for a loop. **Don't waste hours stressing over them.** Focus instead on mastering the vast majority of the pool. If 90% of the material feels easy and a handful of questions confuse you, accept the outlier miss and move on. You only need a 74% to pass—play the odds, stack up the easy wins, and secure the ticket.
4. **Take the Shot:** On testing day, even if you don't feel 100% ready for the General, **take it anyway.** There is zero penalty if you don't pass it.
